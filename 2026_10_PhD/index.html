Inverse problems in cosmology

Solving Inverse Problems : Analytical likelihood


Analytical inversion (Stompor et al. 2009)

\[\mathbf{d} = \mathbf{A}(\boldsymbol\theta)\,\mathbf{s} + \mathbf{n}\]

the data are linear in the signal \(\mathbf{s}\), and the mixing \(\mathbf{A}\) depends on a few parameters \(\boldsymbol\theta\)

\[-2\ln\mathcal{L}(\boldsymbol\theta,\mathbf{s}) = \big(\mathbf{d}-\mathbf{A}\mathbf{s}\big)^{\top}\mathbf{N}^{-1}\big(\mathbf{d}-\mathbf{A}\mathbf{s}\big)\]

for Gaussian noise of covariance \(\mathbf{N}\)

\[\hat{\mathbf{s}}(\boldsymbol\theta) = \left(\mathbf{A}^{\top}\mathbf{N}^{-1}\mathbf{A}\right)^{-1}\mathbf{A}^{\top}\mathbf{N}^{-1}\mathbf{d}\]

setting the derivative with respect to \(\mathbf{s}\) to zero

\[-2\ln\mathcal{L}(\boldsymbol\theta) = -\left(\mathbf{A}^{\top}\mathbf{N}^{-1}\mathbf{d}\right)^{\top}\left(\mathbf{A}^{\top}\mathbf{N}^{-1}\mathbf{A}\right)^{-1}\left(\mathbf{A}^{\top}\mathbf{N}^{-1}\mathbf{d}\right)\]

substituting \(\hat{\mathbf{s}}\) back, up to a constant, leaves a likelihood of \(\boldsymbol\theta\) alone

Analytical inversion requires a model linear in \(\mathbf{s}\) with Gaussian noise

Forward modelling

\[\hat{\mathbf{d}} = \mathcal{A}(\mathbf{s},\boldsymbol\theta)\]

\[-\ln\mathcal{L}(\boldsymbol\theta,\mathbf{s}) \;\propto\; \frac{\lVert \hat{\mathbf{d}} - \mathbf{d} \rVert_2^2}{\sigma^2}\]

Bayesian inference in cosmology

Bayes’ theorem

\[\underbrace{p(\boldsymbol\theta\mid\mathbf{d})}_{\text{posterior}} \;=\; \frac{\overbrace{p(\mathbf{d}\mid\boldsymbol\theta)}^{\text{likelihood}}\;\overbrace{p(\boldsymbol\theta)}^{\text{prior}}}{\underbrace{p(\mathbf{d})}_{\text{evidence}}}\]

\[p(\boldsymbol\theta\mid\mathbf{d}) \;\propto\; p(\mathbf{d}\mid\boldsymbol\theta)\;p(\boldsymbol\theta)\]

Joint probabilistic modelling

\[p(\boldsymbol\theta,\mathbf{s}\mid\mathbf{d}) \;\propto\; \underbrace{p(\mathbf{d}\mid\mathbf{s},\boldsymbol\theta)}_{\text{likelihood}}\;\underbrace{p(\mathbf{s}\mid\boldsymbol\theta)}_{\text{latent prior}}\;\underbrace{p(\boldsymbol\theta)}_{\text{prior}}\]

Fast and robust minimisation

How do we minimise?

The Newton method

\[\boldsymbol\beta_{k+1} = \boldsymbol\beta_k - \mathbf{H}_k^{-1}\,\mathbf{g}_k\] \[\mathbf{g} = \nabla_{\boldsymbol\beta}\left(-\ln\mathcal{L}\right), \qquad \mathbf{H} = \nabla^2_{\boldsymbol\beta}\left(-\ln\mathcal{L}\right)\]

140 GHz, LiteBIRD depth

  • Noisy sky data make the Hessian poorly conditioned and hard to invert
  • Quasi-Newton methods suffer the most since they approximate the inverse Hessian.

Adaptive moment-based methods

Adam is the standard optimiser of machine learning, built for very many parameters and very noisy gradients

\[\boldsymbol\beta_{t+1} = \boldsymbol\beta_t - \eta\,\frac{\hat m_t}{\sqrt{\hat v_t}+\epsilon}\]

\(m_t\), \(v_t\): running means of \(\mathbf{g}\) and \(\mathbf{g}^2\)

AdaBelief scales the step by the spread of the gradient instead

\[v_t \;\longrightarrow\; s_t:\ \text{running mean of }(\mathbf{g} - m_t)^2\]

A noisy direction gets a smaller step

AdaTopK builds on AdaBelief for physical parameters, with a robust handling of parameter bounds

Scaling of AdaTopK

Scaling

  • AdaTopK runs on GPUs with first-order gradients only.
  • It scales well as number of patches grows.

The gravity solver: particle mesh

Gravity through FFTs on a mesh: fast, differentiable, and limited by the mesh resolution

Accuracy of the particle mesh

  • The accuracy of a particle-mesh simulation is limited by the resolution of its mesh
  • At survey volume, a mesh with that resolution does not fit on one GPU (For \(4096^3\) requires 1.6 TB of memory).
  • We need a scalable gravity solver

Distributing the simulation

  • We split the box into pencils, one per GPU
  • jaxDecomp implements a differentiable, distributed FFT for the particle-mesh solver
